# Portfolio case-study draft

Use what exists. A workshop exercise is a useful story about decisions, not proof of business impact.

## Problem

Who was this for? What task was difficult? What was your role, the time available and the scope? Say whether this was fictional, self-directed or part of real work you have permission to discuss.

## Directions

Show today's version if it exists and the alternatives you actually built. For each, explain the different idea in one sentence and its trade-off. Use real captures, not a mock screenshot presented as working code.

## Decision

Name the chosen direction and the reason. Separate your judgment from another person's feedback. Do not claim ownership of decisions someone else made.

## Evidence

Cite the selected observation or source. Describe the participants and method only at a privacy-safe level, and state limits. Use exact quotes only when you have permission for this public use. If no test occurred, say "Not yet tested with users". An AI critique is not a participant study.

## Result

Describe what actually works now. A local prototype is not a launched product. A demo video is evidence of that demonstrated flow, not of adoption. Add metrics only when the person supplies a credible source and permission to use them; otherwise omit them.

## Next

Name one unresolved question and the next test or change. Keep ambitions separate from completed results.

## Review before sharing

- Is your contribution and the agent's role stated accurately?
- Are hypothetical goals separated from measured outcomes?
- Are screenshots, code and assets yours to show?
- Have client details, contacts and participant identities been removed or approved?
- Are links real, and are unfinished parts labeled?
- Does it sound like your own writing?
