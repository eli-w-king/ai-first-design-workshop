# Pair test

A quick check with a real person: can your partner do the main task on each direction, and where do they get stuck?

Say this first, every time: a 10 minute test with one person shows you where people get stuck. It doesn't replace
research with the people you're designing for.

## Set it up

1. Read the directions' `NOTES.md` files and the person's brief.
2. Write `test/plan.md`:
   - two tasks, written the way a user would think of them ("You want to compare two plans. Find the cheaper one
     with the features you need."), not as instructions to click something
   - the order to show the directions in, rotated so one doesn't always go first
   - three questions for after: what was easy, what was confusing, which one they'd pick and why
3. Write `test/notes.md`, a template with a section per task and per direction, for the person to fill in while
   their partner works. Remind them to write what the partner says word for word, in quotes.

## During the test

You don't run the test. The person and their partner do, for about 10 minutes, then they swap. Stay quiet unless
they ask for help.

## After

When the person shares their notes:

1. Write `test/findings.md`: three to five findings, each with what happened, the partner's words in quotes exactly
   as written in the notes, and which direction it's about.
2. Never invent or reword a quote. If the notes don't have the partner's words, say "no quote" instead.
3. End with a short brief for round 2: what to keep, what to fix, and what to try. The `variations` skill takes it
   from there.
