# Pair test

Write short, plain sentences. Do not use em or en dashes in generated copy.

Help the person test a design with another person, then turn what actually happened into a useful next change.

A short partner test can find confusing moments. It cannot establish how common a problem is or replace research with the people the product is for. Say that in the plan and findings.

## Keep this draft-only

- Never recruit, contact, send, launch a study, book a participant, record a person or spend money. Prepare drafts for the person to review and use themselves, even if a study tool is connected.
- Ask before any install, publish or delete. Use only the selected prototype and notes. Do not search accounts or copy someone else's research.
- Agree on a private notes folder outside public repository content. Use participant codes such as P01, not names or contact details. Explain that notes given to the AI may be processed by its provider.
- A quote is evidence only when the notes contain those exact words. Never rewrite a quote to fit a style rule; if needed, omit it and give a clearly attributed summary instead. Never invent participants, quotes, counts, motivations or metrics. Label fictional practice notes and keep them separate from real findings.

## Practice with fictional notes

When the person asks for synthesis, start with the supplied notes rather than planning a new study. Confirm whether they are fictional or real. For fictional inputs, label every output **fictional practice, not customer evidence**. Keep exact supplied quotes traceable to note ids, but never imply that real participants said them.

A small [fictional practice set](references/fictional-practice-notes.md) is available if requested. It is product-agnostic and not the final workshop notes. The curriculum chooses those. Draft themes, contradictions, confidence limits and next design questions; a later redesign is a hypothesis, not a validated result. Do not mix practice data with actual research.

## Draft the plan

Use [the test and notes templates](references/test-templates.md). Ask for the decision, the prototype, the intended audience and two realistic tasks. If a partner is not part of that audience, note the limitation.

1. Write a one-sentence question and an observable success condition for each task.
2. Write tasks as goals, not instructions pointing at the answer. Use the person's own content or clearly labeled sample content.
3. Draft the consent line, including voluntary participation, stopping at any time, what notes contain, who sees them, AI processing if used, and a retention date. No recording is the default.
4. Rotate direction order across participants. With one person, state that order effects remain. Ask what they expected before asking which design they prefer.
5. Suggest a short session with time for introduction, tasks and a debrief. The person moderates and chooses when to swap roles.

If they use a study tool, give them a draft to paste into **the study tool you use**. Do not configure or launch it.

## Capture what happened

The person and participant run the test. Give the observer a blank table separating **observation**, **verbatim words** and **interpretation**. Record task, direction, participant code and note line or timestamp. Do not coach the participant toward success. A participant may skip a question or stop.

## Read the notes

Produce only as many themes as the evidence supports. For each: what happened, the exact source, the participant's words if present, why it may matter, an alternative explanation and a possible design response. Use **no verbatim quote recorded** rather than making one up. Keep contradictions visible. Counts describe only the supplied participants, never the whole audience.

End with a small next-round brief: one thing to keep, one problem to fix, one idea to test, and how another real test would check it. Use [variations](../variations/SKILL.md) only after the person asks to build that next round.

## Return the drafts

Give the plan, notes template, findings and next brief in chat or the agreed private folder. Mark untested ideas as hypotheses. Do not upload notes to a public page, portfolio or PR. For later cleanup, ask before deleting the named notes; removing a local copy does not remove any copies already shared or processed.
