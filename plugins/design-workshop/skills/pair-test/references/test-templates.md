### Consent to adapt and read aloud

"I'm testing this design, not you. Taking part is your choice. You can skip anything or stop at any time. I would like to take written notes using a participant code, not your name. I will not record audio or video. [Say who will see the notes, whether anonymized notes will be processed by an AI provider, and when they will be deleted.] Are you comfortable taking part and with that use of the notes?"

Fill in the brackets before use. Record consent or refusal privately. If the person does not agree to AI processing, do not paste their notes into an agent. Separate permission is needed for recording, attribution, public quotes or a later use of the data. A workshop consent line is not a substitute for any applicable research or privacy requirements.

### Task wording

Give the goal and context, not the control name or a sequence of clicks. For example, a fictional reading-list prototype can ask: "You want to find the article you saved for later and mark it as read." The observer should not say which tab to open.

Ask neutral follow-ups: "What did you expect there?", "What would you try next?" and "What made that easier or harder?" Preference alone is not evidence that a task worked.
