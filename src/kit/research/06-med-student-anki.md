# 06 | Med student who lives in Anki

This student opens Anki before they open their lecture notes. They brought a small practice deck, not their study history. After importing it, they checked the fronts and backs of three familiar cards. They were willing to try a different app with ten cards. They weren't ready to move years of scheduling decisions just because the new home screen looked nicer. "Ten cards is a trial. My whole deck is a commitment."

They understood why a card might come up twice: the second appearance is useful practice. What puzzled them was a finish screen that didn't say whether it was counting different cards or times they'd answered. They sketched three boxes on paper, then drew a second tick in one of them. They'd accept either number if they knew which one they were looking at.

The student made a point of leaving a few cards due when they closed the app. On a busy day, a short review they could repeat tomorrow was better than wiping the queue clean. "Don't turn my backlog into a grade." A deck recommendation was welcome as long as they could choose another one.
