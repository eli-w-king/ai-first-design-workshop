# 02 | Designer learning Japanese

This designer keeps words from a Japanese class in a little notebook. To make a card, they copied the kana for *neko* onto the front and put *cat* on the back. Before turning it over, they paused and said the word under their breath. They looked along the edge of the card for something to press. "The first thing I'd add is a way to hear that word."

They opened a dictionary to listen, but the English meaning was right next to the play button. Once they'd seen it, turning over the card felt pointless. Back in the deck, they drew a small speaker beside the kana on a paper sketch. They wanted to try saying the word once on their own, listen to it, and try again. Sound on every new card would get the order backwards.

Some of their cards run the other way: an English cue on the front and Japanese on the back. They covered that back with their hand. "Don't read the part I'm trying to remember." They'd be happy with a plain button if it respected which side was showing.
